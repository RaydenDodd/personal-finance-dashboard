# Income planning calculation rules

The API and UI use the same pure calculation module, `packages/shared/src/finance-income`. Planning estimates and annualized paycheck averages are distinct from actual posted payroll. No tax estimate changes a bank balance, payroll record or historical salary date.

## Coverage and effective years

The bundled published rules are for **tax year 2026**, checked November 19, 2026. The module never applies them silently to an older year. Missing rules produce unavailable tax/take-home totals, not zero tax. An explicitly enabled future scenario may freeze 2026 assumptions; its status and warnings say **estimated**. A validated, year-matching custom rule set can replace the cache. This is an ordinary wage planning calculator, not a full tax-return preparation engine: AMT, NIIT, investment capital-gain rates, self-employment tax, refundable credits, special tip/overtime deductions, and all state additions/subtractions are outside the automated calculation. Verified adjustments/credits and taxable-income overrides are available for supported scenarios.

Age `0` means unknown. Age-based standard deductions and catch-ups are not inferred. Federal dependent credits require explicit eligible counts; a dependent count alone does not establish eligibility. Married household FICA needs the actual spouse wage share to cap Social Security separately. Per-person actual pay records remain preferable for mixed household payroll deductions.

Traditional 401(k) reduces ordinary taxable income, but does not reduce FICA wages. Roth 401(k) reduces neither. Eligible payroll health deductions and payroll HSA contributions reduce their explicitly selected income/FICA bases. A direct HSA contribution reduces income tax but not FICA. HSA eligibility/coverage is explicit; incompatible health FSA and HSA benefits are not assumed together. The user must include any employer HSA amount in the shared annual limit and must supply any partial-year eligibility adjustment.

Traditional and Roth 401(k) share the $24,500 employee limit; age 50+ catch-up is $8,000, replaced by $11,250 at ages 60–63. Combined regular employee/employer 401(k) contributions are limited to $72,000. Supplied prior-year FICA wages determine the high-earner Roth catch-up treatment. IRAs share $7,500 plus $1,100 age 50+; the cash contribution, deductible portion and Roth eligibility are calculated separately. Contribution caps affect the estimate and generate warnings without rewriting saved inputs. These are individual limits; spousal IRA and multiple simultaneous employer-plan aggregation need separate verified records.

Resident-only wage calculations support the implemented VA/MD/WV reciprocal pairs and DC nonresident wage exemption. Other source-state/credit combinations are unavailable rather than assuming a credit or ignoring a second state's tax. Part-year residency, separate spouse allocation, local credits and special state additions/subtractions need verified inputs. A state taxable-income override supplies the taxable base only; unsupported cross-state credit calculations remain unavailable.

## Cash flow and projections

`net` is gross less all calculated taxes. `takeHome` then subtracts employee 401(k), payroll deductions and payroll HSA. Outside-payroll IRA/direct HSA contributions reduce `afterIra`, not paycheck take-home. Employer 401(k) match and a separate cash-balance/other retirement contribution increase total compensation and retirement only. Employer contributions never inflate spendable pay.

All allocation percentages use total compensation. Every Sankey parent reconciles to its children; a negative disposable balance remains visible and the flow is withheld. The input amount is annual dollars or percentage points (`20` means 20%). Marginal rates are fractions (`0.22`); effective rates are percentage points (`22`). The biweekly/monthly/yearly columns are annual averages, not invented payroll dates. Bonus is a separate input: a salary already containing a bonus must not add it a second time.

Projection scenarios compare end-of-period annual, monthly and biweekly deposits. They convert an effective annual return to each period, grow annual deposits by the entered salary-growth assumption, and discount real values by inflation. A starting balance is market value, not assumed historical principal. Returns are assumptions and not guaranteed; no future tax tables are invented by the projection.
